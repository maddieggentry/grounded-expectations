The definitions behind the numbers

Data & methodology

Reader: A corporate travel manager assessing cancellation exposure when choosing airlines and destination airports. Question: How reliable are the selected airlines, and which airlines and destination airports have the highest cancellation and delay rates?

Source: U.S. Department of Transportation, Bureau of Transportation Statistics, Airline On-Time Statistics and Delay Causes. The source is the uploaded ot_delaycause1_DL (1).zip, whose CSV was verified to match the downloaded January 2018–August 2026 file.

One row: One BTS carrier code at one destination airport in one month. Flight totals include the scheduled arrivals represented in that record, including cancellations. These are monthly aggregates, not individual flight records. scheduled_flights preserves the source arr_flights value; cancelled_flights preserves arr_cancelled.

Four summary measures

  • Scheduled flights: Sum of scheduled_flights over the selected records.
  • Cancelled flights: Sum of cancelled_flights over the same records.
  • Cancellation rate: Cancelled flights divided by scheduled flights. Monthly or airline rates are never averaged.
  • Airlines represented: Distinct count of airline_code in the selected records.

Delay measures

A delayed flight arrives at least 15 minutes late. Delayed flights = sum of delayed_flights (source arr_del15). Delay rate = delayed flights divided by scheduled flights in records with a known delay count. Average delay = sum of arrival_delay_minutes (source arr_delay) divided by delayed flights, using those same records. The source reports delay minutes for delayed flights only. Missing delay counts are preserved as blank and excluded from both numerator and denominator of delay calculations. These records remain in cancellation calculations. The four core summary measures above are unchanged; delay measures supplement them.

A flight may be delayed before it is ultimately cancelled. Here, “delayed” means it arrived at least 15 minutes late; cancelled flights are counted separately. These monthly records do not show the sequence of status changes before cancellation. BTS reporting definitions.

Time and coverage

The report totals, group rankings and dashboard defaults use all available months from January 2018 through August 2026, with all ten airlines, all retained airports and COVID years included. 2018–2025 include twelve months; 2026 includes January–August only. Year comparisons that include 2026 use January–August in every year; other views retain all selected months. Complete-year mode excludes 2026. The 2020–2021 COVID window is an analytical choice and does not assign a cause to any cancellation.

The ten carrier series are a selected comparison set, not every domestic flight or a complete measure of all service sold under each airline brand. Separate regional-carrier codes are excluded to avoid mixing those series with network-labelled records. BTS changes reporting coverage in 2018, so earlier years are excluded. A carrier label does not establish a uniform operating or marketing scope across all airlines. Airline and airport rankings are descriptive, not adjusted for routes, weather, or operating conditions. Hawaiian has no August 2018 record in the source; missing periods are not imputed. Later carrier exits or reporting changes can also reduce available observations.

Airport ranking requires at least 100,000 scheduled flights in the report's selected records. This threshold affects the airport chart only, not the four summary measures. When monthly totals are combined, each flight is weighted equally through the ratio of summed counts.
